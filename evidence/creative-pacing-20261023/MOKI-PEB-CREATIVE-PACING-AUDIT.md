### Underlying causes to correct
1. **Scene duration allocated before dramatic content.** A 14/30-second slot encourages slow reaches and reaction holds. Author beats first; timing follows.
2. **Attempt reset rather than accumulating cost.** The next attempt must inherit a changed prop, position, relationship or resource from the failure. Resetting everything makes effort feel optional.
3. **No visible cost of waiting.** Delivery “upstairs” alone is a task, not pressure. Use a moving crate, last reachable brake, slipping tether, or increasingly difficult rescue—clearly safe cartoon jeopardy, not arbitrary added countdown.
4. **Motion mistaken for story.** Blink/breath/idle wiggle is polish, not an event. A moving body should pursue a target, reveal a thought, evade a consequence or cause the next beat.
5. **All easing treated alike.** Long symmetrical ease curves can make hauling, walking, reaction and impact feel equally soft. Author anticipation, fast action, overshoot and short settle separately; do not globally speed contact-sensitive motion.
6. **Payoff is correct but too remote.** EP030’s ladder irony is usable; most preceding details explain mechanics rather than set up the characters’ overcomplication. Seed a delivery seal so “why not open it first?” is answered visually.

## EP030 production-ready enhanced treatment — “The Easy Way Up”
**100s provisional story. Silent comedy. Same two characters, pulley lift, sealed crate, balcony, visible brake, pre-elevated counterweight and folding/telescoping ladder.** Do not add a third character, extra machine or unseeded rescue prop. Moki believes force solves everything; Peb believes clever shortcuts do. Both must abandon solo cleverness and coordinate. Preserve warm friendship; nobody becomes malicious.

**0–3 — Hook / result before explanation.** Wide: Moki confidently steps onto left lift; the opposite lift shoots upward with Peb clinging to the crate handle. Brake catches at a visibly marked stop before danger. Peb's tiny offended look to Moki. Fast motion is bracketed by readable start/end states, with no unsupported feet.

**3–7 — Goal.** Insert on crate’s intact seal: destination pictogram upstairs, “open after delivery” symbol. Peb points from crate to upstairs dock; Moki gives an overly confident salute. Show the parked iron counterweight high above in the same geography shot—not as a sudden final solution. A brief rattling from the crate invites curiosity, not a second mystery plot.

**7–14 — Attempt 1: muscle.** Moki dismounts to the floor and hauls the **visible separate control rope**, three pulls with differentiated outcomes: tiny move; overshoot checked by Peb; one committed pull reaches upper docking height. Peb protects the crate rather than passively riding. Use three crisp action accents, not seven seconds of identical pulling. Moki visibly locks brake before any celebratory release.

**14–20 — False victory becomes a problem.** Peb reaches for the balcony release but falls visibly short; the gap prevents unloading without the dock pusher. Moki is needed on the lift control. Moki steps aboard to help; with brake deliberately released and external control visible, the load redistribution changes their positions. Peb and box return down; Moki goes up. Moki's triumphant salute freezes into embarrassment. Check masses/energy in previz; do not imply a thought changes gravity.

**20–27 — Attempt 2: shortcut.** Peb gestures “stay”; steps off the right platform at its lower dock, leaving only the box there. Moki’s now-heavier left side descends as the box rises. At the lower stop Peb fixes the ground tether with a clearly shown peg; only then does Moki dismount to the floor. It works—but the box is now upstairs and both are below. Allow one half-second shared look, not a long explanation. Peb grabs the peg to undo it too confidently.

**27–34 — Cost of the shortcut.** Tether releases before Moki is ready; platform begins to reverse. Peb catches the free line, is pulled one short step; Moki catches behind him; together they engage the brake. One visible movement chain: hand → peg → line slack → platform → catch → brake. The crate rocks against its dock stop, not magically off the platform. Stakes have changed from inconvenience to losing their hard-won position. End on their realization: coordinate, don't compete.

**34–41 — Plan 3: cooperate.** Moki hauls while Peb rides, then boards only after braking. Frame the two platforms at equal height with a visible alignment stripe and positive brake lock. No water and no arithmetic captions. Peb points to same-platform boarding; Moki offers his hand. This alignment is actively held by the brake, not declared a free gravitational equilibrium.

**41–49 — Risk / small emotional beat.** Peb hesitates at the gap, sees Moki’s secure stance, commits. Two short efforts: first reach is just short; Moki adjusts stance and extends the other hand; successful assisted hop, landing beside Moki without intersecting bodies. Their weight now shares the left platform; cargo stays right. Let a 0.6–0.9s look communicate trust. Then deliberate brake release.

**49–59 — Payoff to cooperation.** Left platform descends under combined mass; box rises. Peb watches docking stripe; Moki modulates the brake. Peb signals “now”; brake locks. The pre-established dock pusher rolls crate onto balcony and stops it. Clear contact and ownership throughout. Distinguish this success from the earlier false victory by showing the box fully supported on the destination, seal still intact.

**59–66 — Consequence, not reset.** They celebrate together—then look up: cargo delivered, both still below. Peb tries one tiny futile jump toward balcony; Moki catches him at his starting side. This is a brief character gag, not another full platform test. The failed reach redirects Peb’s eyes along the control cord to the elevated counterweight seen at3–7s.

**66–76 — Final solution from seeded resource.** Peb points; Moki recognizes it. Two distinct tasks: Peb readies the receiving side/control indication while Moki pulls the reachable release control. Iron weight visibly rolls from its high shelf onto the now-empty right platform. Right becomes heavier, descends; left rises carrying both. Brake keeps ascent controlled. No weight teleports from floor to top; no automatic winch without power. Keep all releasing hands reachable from their actual platform position.

**76–82 — Climax / coordinated stop.** Rising lift approaches landing; Moki reaches for brake early, Peb stops him, points to alignment stripe, then taps his hand at correct moment. Clean dock. Short bridge extends from the balcony only when locked. They cross briskly, staying inside supports; no slow celebratory parade.

**82–86 — Earned access.** Jointly remove the delivery seal; two clicks, lid opens. A ladder handle pops just above rim. Hold their eyes before revealing the whole prop, not a lengthy lid procedure.

**86–93 — Reversal.** Peb lifts the telescoping ladder; it extends directly to the floor. Wide reconnects floor, ladder, and elaborate lift. Moki looks from ladder to machine, then to Peb. Peb gives exactly the confident salute Moki used at3–7s. Moki’s slow blink is now a purposeful comic hold because it reinterprets the entire story.

**93–100 — Warm button.** Peb descends a few fast, clearly planted rungs, presents the bottom step to Moki as if a grand elevator attendant. Moki starts reaching toward the pulley control out of habit; Peb gently redirects his hand to the ladder. Cut on their agreement, not a full second climb. End card separate; shorten only if channel format permits, never pad story to compensate.

- Canonical script remains immutable. Save adaptation separately with `creative-rewrite` status; no overwrite of EP030 source or published asset.
- Physical plan must be rederived: main rope-length invariant, load direction, all external hauling work, explicit braking at staging/transfer/dock, stored potential energy of elevated weight, stability of crate and dock pusher, access to controls, hand/foot sweeps. Remove bucket only in new adaptation; do not reuse old physics-pass claims.
- Current wide composition is useful for topology. Proposed shot grammar: wide hook; crate/goal insert; medium haul; wide reversal; peg/line insert; wide catch; medium handoff; wide docking; reaction medium; weight insert; wide lift; close reveal; wide irony. At most ~14 purposeful setups, no arbitrary cut quota.
- Performance: eye target precedes reach; decisive silhouette changes at plan reversals; planted push/haul stances; overshoot/settle after load motion; asymmetric reactions. Blinks and breathing never substitute for a beat. Keep face/hand silhouettes clear at mobile viewing size.
- Sound plan (not reviewed): discrete ratchet/pull/lock accents, increasing tension during moving danger, a brief silence before ladder reveal, dry ladder extension accent; no continuous frantic music used to conceal empty action. Listen to the actual encoded result before approving.

## Short-story acceptance rubric (apply before final animation)
Score 0 absent, 1 partial, 2 clearly readable. A numerical score is a triage aid, not proof of fun. No release with a zero in goal, cause/effect, escalation, or payoff.
1. **Hook:** unusual active situation within first ~2s, not title/idle intro.
2. **Goal:** viewer can say what one character wants and what blocks it by ~4s.
3. **Character collision:** Moki and Peb choose differently; not interchangeable operators.
4. **Causal chain:** each attempt produces the next obstacle; story can be told with “because/therefore,” not only “and then.”
5. **Escalation:** at least two qualitatively different changes (risk, scale, responsibility, position, prop function); identical repeats allowed only with a clear comic pattern and changed result.
6. **Surprise:** final turn is seeded but not merely the obvious first gag delayed.
7. **Payoff:** resolves/reframes the original goal and reveals character; end promptly after its readable consequence.
8. **Staging:** core action legible small and muted; viewers track the critical prop, contact and ownership.
9. **Performance:** anticipation/action/reaction have distinct rhythms; reactions affect next decision.
10. **Economy:** removing any 2–3s stretch either loses information/emotion or improves it. Keep intentional tension holds only when a visible threat changes or expectation is actively being manipulated.

**Working short beat template, not mandatory fixed lengths:** 0–2 hook;2–5 goal;5–12 attempt1/backfire;12–22 different attempt/bigger consequence;22–31 surprising solution;31–36 payoff/button. A richer idea may earn45–50s; a single gag may need20–25s. Do not stretch to60s. Never count cuts, blinks, idle motion or repetitive loops as new events.

**Idea upgrade examples:** Hourglass: tampering costs the cheater their own tower, last grain is the true antagonist. Cushion: kindness keeps redirecting the trap until the prankster volunteers for it; one identifiable cushion. Scarf: every concealment makes the original damage harder to hide, repair creates a shared scarf, temptation swaps characters at the button. Sneeze: protecting cards turns suppression into a larger threat; relief, not the huge sneeze, defeats them. These are recommendations—not rendered or validated improvements.

## Creative QA and stop condition
1. Before rig work: 8–12 thumbnail beats; label goal, new information, consequence and emotion for each. Reject repeated unchanged states.
2. Build a cheap timed animatic with scratch effects. Review once continuously at normal speed, first muted, then sound; record actual reviewer identity/time/scope. Ask a fresh reviewer to explain goal, attempts and payoff without notes.
3. Mark every low-information span >~2s in a short or >~3s in a long for a **reasoned review**, not automatic deletion. Suspense and emotional holds can earn time. Identify exactly what changes while it holds.
4. Compare alternative cuts on same story, not playback2×. Keep anticipation/contact readable; cut redundant travel, double reactions, repeated demonstration and administrative completion.
5. Re-render corrected motion; rerun ownership/support/sweep, camera-safe areas, audio sync and decode. Old passes do not transfer to retimed geometry or audio.
6. Review final encoded movie continuously at1× with audio, plus selected full-resolution contact/impact frames. Report timing problems separately from collision defects. No “creative pass” solely from thumbnail coverage.
7. Done only when new animatic and encoded review support the improvement, not when this treatment exists. Audience retention, if later available, can refine—not retroactively prove—the creative hypothesis.
