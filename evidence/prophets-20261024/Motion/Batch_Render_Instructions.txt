Purpose
Produce four complete 1920×1080 films, four native 1080×1920 portrait masters,
and forty separate native portrait Shorts. Every output uses 60 fps and three
genuinely distinct animation samples per output frame. Original narration files
remain immutable. Final videos include the separately produced quiet original
music mix required by project source 04.

had completed before this configuration was measured. Four independent 120-frame
jobs completed in 21.16 seconds including four browser launches and verification;
capture and encoding took 14.54–15.58 seconds per child. Steady capture throughput
was about 30.8 output frames per second across the four children. This exceeded
the 28.5-frame steady throughput of six pages sharing one Node process while

Neither test incurred an out-of-memory event. These are bounded measurements of
E01 at 200 seconds, not a guarantee for every scene. The real full-paragraph run

Frame generation and transport
The browser calls the motion source's composeFrame(t, 60, 3). Its three sample
offsets are 1.3888889, 4.1666667 and 6.9444444 milliseconds after each output
frame's start, all inside its 180-degree shutter interval. The source draws
the scene at each of those times and blends the actual three images. Captions
and the part badge use one frame-start time and are overlaid once, avoiding
double text during shutter blending.

The renderer extracts the blended RGBA pixels into a Blob, sends the binary
Blob through a loopback HTTP stream, and pipes it to ffmpeg. There is no JPEG
encode/decode generation in the default path. An optional --transport jpeg
retains the legacy 0.94-quality JPEG path for comparison. TypedArray request
bodies are deliberately avoided: in this environment they triggered very
expensive protocol copies. A Blob solved that issue.

At most one upload and one newly generated frame are in flight per worker.
The server enforces frame indices, raw byte length, and pipe backpressure.
There is no unbounded queue of images and no per-frame base64/CDP payload.
Only the self-contained local HTML is served; a restrictive CSP prevents it
from requesting outside assets. No narration is uploaded to the local server
or any internet service by this renderer.

Encoding
H.264/libx264, CRF 19, fast preset, yuv420p, 60 fps, closed GOP, 60000-Hz video
time base, explicit limited-range BT.709 conversion/metadata, fast-start MP4.
The final mux watcher encodes AAC 192 kb/s stereo at 48 kHz from the separate
PCM24 music mix. It does not change video frames or original narration WAVs.
The music mix is a distinct artifact; its final waveform includes music and
must not be described as identical to the dry narrator waveform.

Checkpoint and resume rules
Each paragraph and layout becomes a video-only MP4 checkpoint: 80 total.
Every checkpoint starts independently and is suitable for stream-copy concat.
Its receipt records source HTML, audio timing, renderer and encoding settings
fingerprints, output SHA-256, actual frame count and the sample-time evidence.
The exact fingerprints and encoded file hash must match before reuse. The hardened
queue also probes the exact final path and compares frame count, duration,
dimensions, color metadata and before/after SHA. The first resumed queue used
a hash-only reuse check; its exact executed version is preserved in the run
directory. A separate final-path header gate in the muxer covers every current
checkpoint and excludes invalid inputs until repaired. A partial
file is never treated as complete. Keep render_batch.cjs, render_parallel.cjs, and both source HTML files
unchanged during production. Receipts bind source, renderer and settings by
fingerprint. Source equivalence is accepted only through the adoption registry
and its checksum-bound report. Final receipts list the actual source of each
constituent paragraph, including a mix of verified legacy and variant parts.

The independent remux_music_single_pass.cjs watcher creates each final Short when its
portrait checkpoint and episode mix are available. Once ten checkpoints of a
layout are complete, it assembles the full master. It verifies 48 final videos.
Matching mux receipts avoid a second AAC encode on already exported files.
The watcher has a single-process lock and gives each mux attempt its own unique
temporary path. It logs the encoded byte length before probing and complete
decode; only a successful validation is renamed to the final output.

The original guarded muxer remains preserved as remux_music.cjs. After 17
products had completed, it was stopped at a finished pass with no media child
processes. The replacement removes repeated full decodes: one strict full AV
decode supplies the actual frame count through progress=end with passthrough
frame timing, and rejects errors, frame duplication or frame dropping. SHA is
measured before this decode and must equal the committed destination's SHA;
metadata is also checked from that exact final path. Video-only concatenation
uses header/hash checks and explicitly awaits the final product's full decode.
The first real replacement product, E02_P05, passed with 2985 decoded frames,
49.750 seconds, no duplicates/drops, matching hashes, and separate AAC QA.
See QA/Single_Pass_Mux_Adoption.json. Previously completed products are reused
by matching fingerprints and checksums; they are not decoded again.

publish_committed_products.py --watch exposes each committed product immediately
in Export_Verification.json and Incremental_Delivery.json, while the muxer may
still be processing another product in the same pass. It accepts only the 48
expected final paths, validates receipt fields against the immutable frame/audio
plan, and verifies the actual final-file SHA. It never repeats the video decode
or changes media. Its uniquely named atomic ledger writes cannot collide with
the muxer's temporary files. Pending/error fields remain the muxer's last-pass
snapshot; the product list includes all individually committed receipts. The
AAC watcher consumes this list and verifies actual final AAC peak measurements.

Video frames and audio boundaries
Rendering produces 214180 unique video frames over both layouts and 642540
distinct animation samples. The four additional full portrait masters reuse
the portrait Shorts' frames; the 48 delivered files therefore store 321270
frames in total, including that deliberate content reuse. Internal paragraph
cuts are rounded to the nearest
60-fps boundary only after verifying that the new position remains in the
aligned pause between spoken paragraphs. The largest observed adjustment is
8.322 ms. The frame-aligned scene boundaries are injected into the rendering
timeline; original word and caption timings are untouched.

At 44.1 kHz and 60 fps, each video frame is exactly 735 PCM samples. Internal
audio cuts therefore use video_frame × 735; the final end uses the WAV's actual
last sample. Each part begins at the preceding part's exact ending. The forty
parts contain every original PCM sample once, in order. No gap, repetition,
word truncation or generated silence is used. The final video is padded only
by its last incomplete frame, at most 13.334 ms for these recordings.

Portrait Shorts use the exact video checkpoint frames also present in the
portrait master. Their audio is sliced from the separate 48-kHz stereo PCM mix
by sample index (800 samples per video frame), not from compressed AAC. The
final audio end uses the mix's actual last sample. Their separate SRT files are
offset to the same frame-aligned cut. A successful synthetic integration test
verified two 30-frame checkpoints, their stream-copy 60-frame join, exact
0.5/1.0-second AAC durations and complete decoding.

Checks actually implemented
Four unit checks validate forty-part PCM/frame continuity, rejection of a cut
outside a verified pause, rejection of speech across a cut, and preservation
of all original word/caption timestamps. Source WAV hashes are compared with
the immutable audio QA manifest before production. Each checkpoint is decoded
to count frames. Each exported MP4 is checked for dimensions, 60 fps, H.264,
yuv420p, AAC/48-kHz audio, actual duration and complete decode. Intermediate
and final receipts distinguish technical export completion from playback QA.

Review limitations
Technical media checks do not establish listening accuracy or full half-speed
visual review. No such review is claimed by this script or these instructions.
Independent local ASR and the human-visible illustration inspection are separate
production checks. The final delivery report must state the reviews that were
actually performed.
